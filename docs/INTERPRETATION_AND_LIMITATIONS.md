# Interpretation and limitations

## What the comparison supports

At fixed geometry and loading, the calculated open-circuit potential response follows BiOBr > BiOI > BiOCl. The same ranking is obtained with the original reference parameterization, with consistently electronic dielectric tensors, and with consistently total dielectric tensors. The result therefore supports a robust relative electromechanical trend within the present model.

BiOBr combines a comparatively compliant out-of-plane mechanical response with strong piezoelectric coupling and moderate dielectric screening. This balance provides a physically plausible electromechanical contribution to the experimentally observed optimum of BiOBr. The calculation is best used together with PFM, trapping/spectroscopic evidence, and catalytic kinetics rather than as a stand-alone explanation.

## What the comparison does not establish

The model does not prove that the calculated potential is fully retained in an illuminated catalyst or at a solid-liquid interface. It does not calculate carrier lifetime, band bending, reactive oxygen species yield, adsorption energy, surface reaction barriers, or pollutant degradation rates. Statements about charge separation or catalytic enhancement should therefore be phrased as implications consistent with the field calculation, not as direct FEM outputs.

## Parameter uncertainty

The absolute potential is sensitive to the dielectric tensor. The original parameterization uses the total static dielectric response for BiOCl but electronic values for BiOBr and BiOI. The sensitivity table resolves this inconsistency transparently and shows that the rank order is stable even though the magnitudes are not.

The nonzero $e_{31}$, $e_{32}$, and $e_{33}$ values in the configuration are supplied inputs. They could not be traced to the archived first-principles output examined during this project, which reported zero piezoelectric tensors. Absolute response values should not be presented as intrinsic constants until these coefficients have a citable source or are independently validated.

## Geometrical and physical idealizations

The model treats each compound as a homogeneous, defect-free 5 nm single domain. Real powders and pellets can contain grains, preferred orientation, porosity, contacts, surface reconstruction, halogen vacancies, adsorbates, and defect dipoles. These features may change both local stress transfer and polarization.

The stationary calculation also omits ultrasonic frequency, inertia, acoustic impedance, liquid loading, cavitation, free-carrier redistribution, and electrolyte screening. A future dynamic model should use measured particle dimensions, solvent properties, excitation frequency and pressure amplitude, and experimentally constrained damping.

## Recommended reporting language

Appropriate: "The finite-element comparison predicts the largest open-circuit piezopotential response for BiOBr under identical idealized loading, and the ordering is retained under two consistent dielectric conventions."

Avoid: "The simulation proves that BiOBr has the highest photocatalytic rate" or "the PFM drive corresponds to 100 MPa."
